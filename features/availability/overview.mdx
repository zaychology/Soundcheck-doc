Soundcheck's availability system gives you the information you need to staff a gig before the invitations go out. Instead of texting your crew one by one and hoping for fast replies, you can check who's marked themselves free, send targeted requests for a specific date, and read the whole org's schedule on a shared calendar — then staff from what you see.

## Three availability layers

Soundcheck breaks availability into three connected layers that work together from the moment a date appears on your radar to the moment a crew member accepts an invitation.

**Personal availability** is what each crew member owns. They mark the dates and times they can or cannot work, and that data feeds every other surface in the system. Schedulers see it; the crew calendar reflects it; availability requests reconcile against it automatically.

**Availability requests** are how you get an explicit yes/no for a specific gig date before you commit to a lineup. Send a request to one person or a filtered list of positions, set the date and time window, and collect responses in one place — no chasing required. Responses feed back into each member's personal calendar automatically.

**The crew calendar** is the org-wide view schedulers use to spot booking conflicts and coverage gaps before they build a team. It shows who's available, who's already booked on another show, and who hasn't responded yet, all in one timeline.

## Why this matters for staffing

Double-booking is the most common crew staffing mistake, and it usually happens because a scheduler didn't know someone had already accepted a different gig. Soundcheck prevents that by surfacing conflict blocks on the crew calendar the moment a member accepts any invitation or offer — not just when they're listed on a roster. That distinction keeps your view accurate.

The availability layer also speeds up staffing significantly. When you open **Manage Team** on a new gig, you can see which members holding the positions you need are actually free that day. That turns an hour of outreach into a few minutes of confirmation.

## How availability connects to invitations and crew offers

Availability is not a gate — it's a signal. A member marked unavailable can still receive an invitation; Soundcheck surfaces the conflict and lets you decide. But in practice, the availability layer tells you who to invite before you ask, so by the time invitations go out the roster is nearly set.

For crew offers (open shifts posted to a shortlist of eligible people), the crew calendar's position filter shows exactly who holds the right role and is free — letting you direct the offer at the strongest candidates instead of broadcasting to everyone.

Once an invitation or offer is accepted, that member's time is automatically blocked on the crew calendar, keeping the picture accurate for every subsequent gig you staff.
