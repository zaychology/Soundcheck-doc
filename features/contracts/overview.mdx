Soundcheck keeps your contracts in the same place as your schedule, your crew, and your finances. You send an agreement, signers get a request, and Soundcheck records every status change — Sent, Viewed, Signed, Declined — so nothing falls through the cracks on the way to an executed deal. E-signatures are powered by SignWell, and on Enterprise plans you can send contracts under your own logo and colors for a fully on-brand signing experience.

<Note>
  Contracts are rolling out per organization. If you don't see contract actions on your gigs, contact support at [info@soundchecklive.io](mailto:info@soundchecklive.io) to enable them for your org.
</Note>

## Contract lifecycle

Every contract moves through a clear set of statuses:

| Status | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Draft** | The contract is prepared but not yet sent. Edit freely before you send. |
| **Sent** | Signature requests have gone out. You're waiting on signers. |
| **Signed** | Every required signer has completed. The executed document is attached to the gig. |
| **Declined** | A signer declined. They're routed back so you can follow up, adjust terms, and resend. |

## Sending a contract

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the gig's contracts area">
    Navigate to the gig and find the Contracts tab or section. Tying the contract to the gig means its status lives alongside the roster, run of show, and finances it covers.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Attach the agreement">
    Add or upload the contract document you want to send. Give it a name that makes it easy to identify later in the gig record.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add signers">
    Add each person who needs to sign. Each signer needs a name and email address. Soundcheck tracks every signer's status individually so you always know who's still outstanding.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Send for signature">
    Send the contract. Each signer receives a SignWell signature request by email. The contract status moves to **Sent** on the gig.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Track to completion">
    Watch each signer's progress from the gig. When the last required signer completes, the contract status flips to **Signed** and the executed copy is stored on the gig record automatically.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Tracking signature status

The gig's contract view shows every signer and where they stand. You can see at a glance who has received the request, who has opened it, who has signed, and who still needs to act. Because contracts live on the gig, an outstanding signature shows up in the gig's open items alongside unpaid crew and unconfirmed invitations — nothing hides in a separate tool.

## When a signer declines

A declined contract doesn't dead-end your workflow. When a signer declines, Soundcheck routes them back to your organization portal so you can reach out, adjust the terms, and resend — keeping the entire negotiation tied to the gig instead of scattering it across email threads. Once you've resolved the issue, resend the contract to move it back to **Sent**.

## Signed contracts on the gig record

Once every required signer completes, the executed agreement is automatically attached to the gig. It lives there alongside the run of show, files, and payment records — so anyone on your team can pull the signed contract from the gig without hunting through email attachments or cloud folders.

## White-label contract branding

On the **Enterprise** plan, your logo and colors appear throughout the signing experience your clients and crew see — so the agreement reads like it came from your company, not a third-party tool. White-label branding covers the email invitation, the signing window, and the completed-document view. It is provisioned by the Soundcheck team; contact [info@soundchecklive.io](mailto:info@soundchecklive.io) to request setup once your organization is on Enterprise.

<Tip>
  Pair white-label contract branding with the [client portal](/features/client-portal/overview) for a fully on-brand client experience: your logo and colors carry through both the portal and the contract signing window, so everything the client touches looks like it came from you.
</Tip>
