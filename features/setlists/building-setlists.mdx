---

A great setlist does two things: it fits your time slot and carries the crowd through the right energy arc. Soundcheck's drag-and-drop builder handles the math while you handle the music. This guide walks you through the full process from opening Music Management to handing your crew a complete, gig-attached setlist.

<Steps>

  <Step title="Open Music Management">
    Click **Music Management** in the main navigation. You'll land on the **Setlists** tab, which lists all your existing setlists and templates. Click **+ Create Setlist** to start a new one.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Name your setlist and choose its type">
    Give your setlist a clear, searchable name — for example, *Friday Night at Blue Note* or *Smith Wedding – June 14*. Then choose whether it's a **template** (reusable, not tied to a show) or a **gig setlist** (attached to a specific event). You can change this later, but picking the right type now keeps your list tidy.

    <Note>
      If you plan to run the same set shape repeatedly — a three-set club format, a ceremony-to-dancing wedding structure — start with a template. Duplicate it for each new booking rather than rebuilding from scratch.
    </Note>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add your sets">
    Sets are the major sections of your performance. Click **+ Add Set** and name each one. Common naming patterns:

    | Event type | Typical sets |
    |---|---|
    | Club gig | Opening, Main, Encore |
    | Wedding | Ceremony, Cocktail Hour, Dinner, Dancing |
    | Corporate | Background, Performance, Dance |
    | Festival | Main Set |

    Add a set for each section your show needs. You can reorder sets at any point by dragging their header.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add songs from the library">
    Inside each set, click **+ Add Song**. A search panel opens your Song Library — type a title or artist name to find what you need, then click the song to add it. Repeat until the set is full.

    If a song isn't in your library yet, select **Create New** from the same panel to add it on the fly. It saves to the library and drops straight into the set.

    <Tip>
      Build your Song Library before you start building setlists. A well-stocked library makes setlist assembly a drag-and-drop exercise instead of a data-entry session. See [Song Library](/features/setlists/song-library) for details.
    </Tip>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add per-song notes">
    Click any song in the builder to expand its details. The **Notes** field is where you leave cues for the band or crew: *"Extended solo section"*, *"Skip the bridge live"*, *"Drummer counts in"*. Notes are visible to the assigned crew on mobile on show day.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Reorder for energy flow">
    Drag any song to a new position within its set, or drag it into a different set entirely. Use the set header handle to reorder the sets themselves. A typical energy arc:

    - **Opening set** — medium energy, warm the room
    - **Build** — increase energy progressively
    - **Peak** — high-energy centrepiece songs
    - **Encore** — end strong or provide a memorable closer

    Running time recalculates instantly as you move songs, so you can chase the right pacing and timing at the same time.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Check total running time against your slot">
    The setlist header shows the grand total; each set header shows its subtotal. Compare those figures to your contracted time slot before you finalize. If you're over, move songs to a "cuts" list in the notes or remove them. If you're under, now is the time to pad — not mid-show.

    ```text
    Setlist: Friday Night at Blue Note
    ├── Set 1: Opening (45 min)
    │   ├── Take Five
    │   └── Autumn Leaves
    ├── Set 2: Main (60 min)
    │   └── All Blues
    └── Set 3: Encore (15 min)
        └── A Night in Tunisia
    ```

    <Warning>
      Songs without a duration entered show as `—:—` and are excluded from the running total. Check your Song Library and fill in any missing durations before relying on the timing numbers.
    </Warning>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Attach the setlist to a gig">
    When the setlist is ready, link it to the gig so the whole team can find it in one place.

    **From the setlist builder** — use the setlist's action menu to associate it with a gig.

    **From the gig detail page** — go to the **Setlist** section and click **Select Setlist**, then choose this setlist from the list. The setlist is now visible to every crew member assigned to that gig, on web and mobile.

    <Note>
      You can also create a brand-new setlist directly from the gig's Setlist section. It builds in the same builder and links automatically when you save.
    </Note>
  </Step>

</Steps>

## Duplicating and templating

Once you have a setlist that works, save yourself the rebuild next time:

- **Duplicate** — open the setlist, click **Actions → Duplicate**, rename the copy, and adjust. All sets, songs, and notes copy over; the gig association does not.
- **Templates** — keep a clean "master" setlist (e.g., *Template – Three-Set Club*) that you duplicate for each new booking. Name templates clearly so they stand out in the list.
