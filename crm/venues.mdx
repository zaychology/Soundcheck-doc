Your venue directory is your organization's shared list of every place you work. Save a venue once — name, address, contact, stage specs, load-in details, and access notes — and link it to any gig. Every crew member on that show sees the right information without anyone retyping it. When you update a venue record, every future gig using it picks up the change automatically.

<Info>
  Venues live under **Crew & contacts → Venues** in the sidebar and are available on **Team** and **Enterprise** plans. On **Free**, the Venues section may be hidden or show a lock icon. If you don't see Venues, [contact support](mailto:info@soundchecklive.io).
</Info>

## Open the venue directory

Navigate to **Crew & contacts → Venues** in the sidebar. Browse your saved venues, use the search bar to find a specific place, or filter by type, rating, or status. Select any venue card to open its detail page.

Owners, admins, and gig coordinators can create and manage venue records.

## Add a venue

When you create a new venue, start by searching for it by name. Soundcheck pulls details from Google Business listings to give you a head start.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Search for the venue">
    Type the venue's name in the search field. Soundcheck queries Google Business listings and displays matching results.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select a result">
    Pick the correct listing. Soundcheck auto-fills the **name, address, phone number, website, and photo**, and infers a **venue type** from Google's categories.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add your own specs">
    Fill in what Google doesn't know: capacity, stage dimensions, house tech, load-in route, access codes, and contact details specific to your booking relationship. Then save.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Linking to Google is optional. If a venue isn't in Google's listings, type a name and fill everything in by hand. Venues pulled from Google show a **record source** badge so your team knows where the details originated.
</Note>

## Browse the venue directory

Each venue appears as a card showing its photo, type, address, aggregate rating, and quick counts for capacity, past gigs, and upcoming gigs. Cards come in two states:

| State | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Saved** | The venue is in your organization's directory. Click through to the full detail page to edit it. |
| **Directory match** | A shared record not yet in your directory. Click **Save** to add it, after which it becomes editable. |

## The venue detail page

Each saved venue has a detail page with everything your team needs in one spot. Most fields edit inline — click a value, update it, and it saves automatically.

### Header and quick stats

The top of the page shows the venue name, type, address, and aggregate **rating** (a star and average score with the review count behind it). A stats row surfaces **capacity, past gigs, upcoming gigs, and the next event date**. Use **Directions** to open the venue in Google Maps.

### Contact

Store the venue's booking or production contact on file: name, role, phone, email, and website.

### Stage and logistics

Everything a crew needs to plan a load-in:

| Field | Use it for |
|---|---|
| Stage size | Stage dimensions |
| Dressing rooms / Green room | Backstage availability |
| Wi-Fi | Network name and password or guest portal URL |
| House tech | PA, console, lighting rig, and other in-house gear |
| Load-in / Load-out | Route from the dock to the stage; strike timing |
| Dock / Elevator / Parking | Loading access, freight details, crew and truck parking |
| Access code | Door, gate, or elevator code (**visible only to your organization**) |
| Public notes | Details useful to anyone working this venue |

<Info>
  Load-in and access fields — access code, dock, load-in, load-out, elevator, and parking — automatically flow onto every gig linked to this venue. Enter them once here and every crew member on every future show sees them.
</Info>

### Amenities and restrictions

Tag the venue with **amenities** (parking, catering, loading dock) and **restrictions** (curfew, decibel limit, no pyro) so they're visible at a glance and searchable across your directory.

### Private fields

Some details should stay with your team and not appear on any shared profile:

- **Private status** — a working label such as *preferred*, *backup*, or *avoid*.
- **Private notes** — contact preferences, settlement notes, or anything internal.
- **Record sources** — where the venue's data came from (Google, a connected system, or *Manual record*).

### Linked customers and related gigs

The detail page lists **customers** associated with the venue and the full history of **gigs** linked to it. Jump straight from a venue to any event you've booked there.

## Search and filter the venue directory

Use the search bar to find venues by name, address, or type. Filter by status (saved vs. directory match), rating, or tags like amenities and restrictions. If you work across a wide range of venue types, filters keep the directory manageable as it grows.

## Link a venue to a gig

Connect a saved venue to a gig from the gig's **Venue** tab.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the gig's Venue tab">
    On any gig, select the **Venue** tab.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a venue from your directory">
    Select a saved venue. The gig immediately shows the venue on a map along with its contact and specs.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review load-in and access details">
    The six shared load-in and access fields flow in from the venue record automatically. Your crew sees them without anyone copying or retyping.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Override details for a specific show

The venue record sets the default, but individual shows can differ. On the gig's Venue tab you can:

- **Override any load-in or access field for this show only.** The override sits on the gig; the shared venue record stays unchanged. An **override count** badge appears so everyone knows some details differ from the usual setup.
- **Add "this show only" notes** for anything crew need to know tonight — a blocked dock, an alternate entrance, a changed access code.

<Info>
  Load-in details, access information, and show notes are visible to everyone working the gig, including crew who can't edit the venue directory. The people on the ground always get what they need.
</Info>

## Keep notes on a venue

Use **public notes** for anything useful to anyone working the venue — house quirks, parking tips, or curfew reminders. Use **private notes** for internal team context that shouldn't be visible in shared gig views, like negotiation history or settlement notes.

Ratings accumulate from gig reviews over time, giving your team a quick read on reliability and experience at a glance.
